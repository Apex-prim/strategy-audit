# Before the backtest: can this history answer the question at all?

A barrier claim — *price reaches +X before −X more often than a coin would* —
is settled by races, not by candles. A race starts somewhere and ends the
moment one of the two barriers is touched. The next independent race starts
where the last one finished.

Backtests report candles. Six years of daily bars is about 2,200 of them,
which sounds like plenty. The number that decides whether your result can
exist is different, and for wide barriers it is small enough to be
embarrassing.

Numbers below are produced by [`resolvable.py`](resolvable.py) from
[`resolvable_run.json`](resolvable_run.json).

## Read the last column first

At every barrier width, on both symbols, the history is short by a multiple —
not by a little. And the comparison is generous: the required sample is
computed against the rate that was *observed*, which is the most favourable
alternative you could pick, and picking it after the fact is already cheating
in your own favour.

If the honest alternative — say 0.55, a rate that would be a real edge —
needs 782 races and your widest barrier gives you 13, the question is not
whether the strategy works. The question does not have an answer in this
data, and no amount of care in the backtest creates one.

## The trap hiding in the share-up column

The proportion of races that resolve upward rises with barrier width. On BTC
it goes 0.55 at 500 bps to 0.77 at 5000 bps, and it is tempting to read that
as "wide barriers are more predictable."

Look at the median duration in the same row. It goes from days to **months**.
A race that takes five months to resolve is not measuring direction; it is
measuring drift, and over a period when the asset rose a lot, drift wins by
construction. Randomise the signs of the daily returns and the rising column
mostly survives — which is exactly why a sign-randomisation control belongs
in any test like this, and why a rising column without one proves nothing.

That is the whole reason this file exists next to a backtest, not inside one.

## What to do with a "short by 4x" row

Three honest options, and one dishonest one.

- **Say you cannot answer it.** Underpowered is a result. It is not the same
  as "no effect", and writing it down as "no effect" is how a live question
  gets buried.
- **Narrow the barrier** until the races are numerous enough — accepting that
  you are now answering a different, smaller question.
- **Wait for data.** Slow, and often the only correct answer.

The dishonest one is to run it anyway, find p < 0.05 somewhere among the
barriers you tried, and report that one. With five barriers and two symbols
you have ten chances at a coin flip; one of them will oblige.

## Reproduce it

```
python resolvable.py --fetch    # downloads daily candles, recounts races
python resolvable.py            # re-renders the block above
```

Barrier widths and the alternatives in the power table are fixed in the
source. They were chosen before the run and are not searched over.

## What this does not do

It says nothing about whether a strategy is good. It only says whether your
data is capable of telling you. Those are different questions, and the second
one is cheaper — which is a reason to ask it first, not a reason to skip it.
