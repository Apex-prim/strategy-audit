# Buying the drawdown: a real effect you cannot take

[DCA.md](DCA.md) measured what averaging into a position does across 895
public strategies. This is the question sitting underneath it. After price
has already fallen by some amount, is the next move better than average?

The answer changes sign depending on how deep you cut. That is not a
subtlety. It is the whole finding, and quoting either half alone is how a
real effect becomes a wrong headline — including in my own notes, which is
where this started.

Every number below comes from [`depth.py`](depth.py) reading
[`depth_run.json`](depth_run.json). None of them are typed by hand.

## What the bands say

Shallow drawdowns are worse than an average hour. A 100–200 bps dip on BTC
is followed by a 24-hour move about 9 bps below baseline, and the pattern
holds through 300–500. If you buy small dips you are buying an anti-effect,
and there are enough observations — twelve thousand hours in the first band
alone — that this is not noise.

Then at 500 bps the sign flips. BTC goes from −7.6 to **+55.0** bps of
excess, ETH from −21.8 to **+23.8**. Same instrument, same window, one
threshold apart.

This is the part I had wrong. My own registry carried the line *"digging
rebounds means digging an anti-effect"*, which was a fair summary of the
shallow bands and a false statement about the class. The original study had
even flagged the deep tail as unmeasured — 160 observations, no verdict —
and the caveat did not survive into the headline. The tail here has 3,386
hours on BTC and 6,350 on ETH.

## The objection that has to be answered first

2020 through 2026 was mostly an uptrend. Any rule that says *buy after a
fall* will look good in a market that spends most of its time recovering.
That objection cannot be argued away, so it is measured.

Look at the by-year rows. On BTC, 2022 — the year the price lost about two
thirds — has the **largest** episode excess of any year in the sample:
+174.6 bps against a baseline that is itself negative that year. On ETH the
same year gives +193.4. The effect is not the bull market wearing a costume.
It is strongest exactly where the bull-market explanation predicts it should
vanish.

At that point this looks like a finding.

## And this is where it dies

An episode is a crash: a contiguous run of hours where the drawdown stays
past the threshold, median length 14 hours on BTC and 17 on ETH. The
"episode mean" averages the forward return over every hour in that run —
including the hours near the bottom.

You do not know where the bottom is while you are standing in it.

The only entry you can actually execute is the first hour the condition
fires. That number is reported separately, and it is:

- **BTC: −29.1 bps of excess**, 95% CI [−93.2, +35.1], 51.1% of episodes up
- **ETH: +2.1 bps of excess**, 95% CI [−49.3, +53.5], 54.1% up

Zero, on both, with confidence intervals that swallow anything you might
want to do with them. Round-trip cost is about 4 bps as a maker, plus
funding — so even the point estimates do not clear the bill.

The gap between +129 and −29 on the same data is not a rounding difference.
It is the distance between a quantity that exists and a quantity you can
reach. The rebound is real, it survives the worst control I could put it
through, and it is positioned in the middle of a fall whose midpoint is only
knowable afterwards.

## Why this is worth writing down

Three separate things here are ordinary mistakes that look like results:

**A headline that outgrew its measurement.** The shallow bands were measured
and killed. The deep tail was explicitly not measured. The summary covered
both. Nobody lied; the caveat just weighed less than the sentence.

**A control that was skipped because the answer seemed obvious.** "It is
just the uptrend" is the correct first objection and it happens to be wrong
here. Not running it would have left a true finding buried under a plausible
dismissal — the mirror image of the first mistake.

**A unit of analysis chosen for convenience.** Per-hour, per-episode, and
first-hour-only are three different questions. Two of them have no tradeable
meaning. Reporting the flattering one is not fraud, it is just not answering
the question anyone cares about.

If you are running a strategy that adds into drawdowns, the middle number is
the one describing your fills, and the bottom one is the one describing your
account.

## Reproduce it

```
python depth.py --fetch     # downloads ~58k hourly candles per symbol, recomputes
python depth.py             # re-renders the block above from depth_run.json
```

No API key is needed; the candles come from the public endpoint. The
threshold of 500 bps was fixed before the run and no search was made over
it — a search would have found a better one, and that better one would have
meant nothing.

## What this does not show

It does not show that no entry rule inside a deep drawdown works. It shows
that the naive one — enter when the condition first fires — does not. A rule
conditioning on something else (confirmation, time-in-episode, a second
instrument) is a different question, and would need its own pre-registration
and its own out-of-sample data, not this one.

It also covers two symbols and one venue. Nothing here says anything about
equities, or about a horizon other than 24 hours.
